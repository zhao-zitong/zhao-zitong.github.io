RESEARCH

  • What Large Funds Know: Fund Scale and the Production of Information April 2026
    with Dora Horstman and Shimeng Wang
    • Presentations: TAMU Brownbag Seminar 2026 (scheduled); FMA 2026 (scheduled)

    • This paper studies which mutual fund skill scales. Using holdings-based decompositions of performance, we find that stock picking exhibits strong decreasing returns to scale, whereas market timing is comparatively scalable. A one-standard-deviation increase in fund size reduces picking alpha by 6.48%/year while leaving timing alpha unchanged. As funds grow, the composition of dollar value added shifts from stock picking toward market timing. Consistent with managers reallocating attention from capacity-constrained firm-specific signals toward scalable aggregate signals, the trades of growing funds become less predictive of firm-specific outcomes. Their trade-implied shifts in market exposure, by contrast, remain predictive of market returns. Fund scale changes not only how much funds earn but what they know.

  • What's in a Green Name? Evidence from ESG Mutual Funds October 2024
    • Presentations: TAMU Brownbag Seminar 2024; FMA 2025; SFA 2025

    • This paper examines the fire sale behaviors of ESG mutual funds, categorized by portfolio and by name, in response to substantial outflows. The analysis reveals that investors exhibit no sensitivity to the underperformance of ESG-named funds. These funds are less likely to engage in fire sales of high-ESG stocks, instead opting to maintain or even increase their allocations to such stocks. Moreover, high-ESG stocks experience lower flow-induced selling pressure, with this effect being further mitigated when the stocks are more heavily owned by ESG funds. The findings suggest that the weak flow-performance relationship enables ESG-named funds to retain high-ESG stocks, even at the expense of financial performance.
